How the model works

The Huff model treats every store as a magnet. Bigger stores pull harder, and pull fades with distance. Each neighborhood splits its spending across all the stores in a category in proportion to their pull.

Pnew = Unew / (Unew + ΣUexisting),   where U = sizeα / distanceβ Predicted visits = Σ over neighborhoods of Pnew × that neighborhood’s historical visits in the category. Market share = predicted visits ÷ total category demand in Worcester.

  1. Calibrate α and β

    A grid search fits size sensitivity (α) and distance decay (β) for each business category against observed visit flows. Only categories with correlation above 0.25 and enough history are kept.

  2. Precompute the competition

    The sum of existing competitor pull is stored for every category and neighborhood, so scoring a new site only needs the new store’s own pull. That took queries from file scans to sub-millisecond lookups.

  3. Score the candidate

    Straight-line distances from the site to all 149 neighborhood centroids, in a projected coordinate system (UTM zone 19N), give capture probabilities, predicted visits, and market share.

Keeping the numbers honest

A site-selection tool is only useful if people can tell how much to trust it.

The assistant never invents results

In the “AI as controller” design, every predicted visit, share, and distance comes from the engine’s tool output. The language model only gathers inputs and explains.

Confidence labels on every answer

Results open with Calibrated, Rough estimate, or No data, and flag a modest fit when calibration correlation is below about 0.4.

Data gaps aren’t bad locations

When a category has no demand data, the zeros are reported as a gap rather than as a poor site.

Assumptions stated plainly

Distances are straight-line from neighborhood centroids, and neighborhoods without coordinates are excluded and reported.

Data: Worcester, MA census block groups (2010 boundaries), points of interest, and origin-to-store visit counts from the project dataset. Visits are allocated from historical visit counts, so treat them as relative estimates rather than exact forecasts.

Built by Jirapa, Zexuan, and Yixuan He with Python, Flask, Azure SQL, Azure OpenAI, Leaflet, and Chart.js. This page’s demo is written in JavaScript with D3.js.
